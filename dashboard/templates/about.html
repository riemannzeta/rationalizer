About & Methodology

News Rationalizer is an experimental analysis tool that measures emotional valence patterns in journalism to identify balanced versus biased reporting across topics.

The Mathematical Analogy

In complex number theory, a conjugate is a number that, when multiplied with its pair, eliminates the imaginary component and yields a real number:

(a + bi) × (a - bi) = a² + b²

Similarly, we can "rationalize" news consumption by pairing articles from authors with complementary emotional valences—combining a positive-leaning perspective with a negative-leaning one on the same topic. The goal is to approach more comprehensive, balanced understanding by exposing ourselves to opposing emotional framings.

Note: "Balanced" does not necessarily mean "accurate" or "unbiased." This is a measure of emotional tone, not factual correctness or journalistic quality.

Analysis Pipeline

1. Data Collection

Articles are collected from diverse news sources via RSS feeds, including: BBC, Reuters, The Guardian, NPR, Al Jazeera, The Hill, Axios, and TechCrunch. We extract title, author, publication date, and content.

2. Topic Categorization

Each article is classified into one or more topic categories using keyword matching:

    {% for cat_id, cat_info in categories.items %}
  • {{ cat_info.label }}: {{ cat_info.description }}
    • {% endfor %}

Articles can belong to multiple categories with confidence scores. Only articles scoring above 15% confidence in a category are included.

3. Sentiment Analysis

We apply a pre-trained RoBERTa sentiment model (cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest) to analyze emotional valence. Each article receives:

  • Title valence score (-1 to +1)
  • Content valence score (-1 to +1)
  • Combined overall valence (weighted 30% title, 70% content)

4. Author Profiling

For each author with at least 3 articles in a category, we calculate:

  • Average valence per category
  • Valence variance within categories (consistency)
  • Cross-category variance (tone variation between topics)
  • Balance score: 1 / (1 + cross_category_variance × 10)

Limitations & Caveats

What Could Invalidate These Results?

Insufficient sample sizes: Authors need multiple articles per category for meaningful metrics. We require minimum 3 articles per category.

Model bias: The sentiment model was trained on Twitter data, which may not generalize perfectly to news article language. It may misinterpret journalistic objectivity as neutrality, or miss subtle emotional cues in formal writing.

Topic misclassification: Keyword-based categorization can misfire. An article mentioning "nuclear" in passing might be incorrectly categorized as being about nuclear energy.

Conflating sentiment with bias: Negative emotional valence doesn't mean "biased" or "wrong." A critical article about healthcare failures may be both accurate and negatively-valenced. Positive framing can also be misleading.

Missing context: We analyze tone, not truthfulness, depth, or quality. A "balanced" author by our metric might simply avoid emotional language while still being one-sided in their analysis.

How to Use This Tool

Explore category pages to see the emotional spectrum of coverage on a topic. Look for "complementary pairs" of authors with opposing valences to read both sides.

Check author profiles to understand individual writing patterns across topics. Authors with high balance scores maintain consistent tone; low scores suggest topic-dependent framing.

Remember: This is a heuristic, not a truth machine. Use it as one input among many when evaluating media sources. Emotional valence is interesting but doesn't replace critical thinking about accuracy, sourcing, and argumentation quality.

Falsifiability & Testing

To test whether this system is working correctly, we include:

  • Sample size indicators (article counts, confidence scores)
  • Access to individual articles so you can manually verify sentiment classifications
  • Variance metrics to show when an author's tone is inconsistent (low confidence in categorization)
  • This methodology page explaining our assumptions and known failure modes

If you find cases where the sentiment analysis seems obviously wrong, or where categorization misses the point of an article, those are valuable data points about the system's limitations.
